3. Clip by clip: what plays, and what you say

0 · Intro — c0_intro_card.mp4

0:00–0:09

Title card, fading in and out: Smart India Hackathon 2026 · ISRO · SIH26175, DepthWizard, the problem statement's title, Team Dev Up, team ID 129655, theme Disaster Management.

Hello, we're Team Dev Up. This is DepthWizard — our solution to ISRO's problem statement on single-view height estimation and 3D flythrough.

1 · Hook — c1_hook_sikkim.mp4

0:09–0:24

Slow orbit and push-in over the Sikkim valley. No interface on screen.

One satellite image. No stereo pair, no laser survey, no new flight. This is Sikkim — rebuilt in 3D from a single picture, by DepthWizard.

2 · The problem — c2_problem_omaha.mp4

0:24–0:44

Opens straight down on Omaha (it looks like a flat photo), then tilts into 3D from about 4 s.

From above, a satellite image is flat. Height normally needs stereo imagery or LiDAR — expensive, and impossible for the decades of archive India already holds. DepthWizard estimates height in metres from one image, and tells you how sure it is.

3 · Upload — c3_upload.mp4

0:44–1:31

0–7 s the sample window opens · 7–11 s a file is chosen from the computer · 11–20 s the progress bar (the minute of processing is already cut) · 20–47 s the result opens and is explored, download links in the left panel.

0–11 s, while choosingAnyone can try it. Pick one of our sample images, or any image from your own computer — a GeoTIFF, a PNG or a JPG. 11–20 s, over the progress barIt runs on an ordinary CPU server, with no GPU. This image takes about a minute; we've cut the wait. 20–47 s, over the resultBecause this file carries coordinates, the heights are anchored to open Copernicus terrain — a real surface in metres above sea level, exactly what ISRO evaluates. Every result downloads as standard GeoTIFFs: the DSM, height above ground, and a per-pixel uncertainty map, ready for QGIS — plus a quick-look picture anyone can open.

4 · Explore and measure — c4_flythrough_jacksonville.mp4

1:31–1:46

Low, sweeping fly-through over a park in Jacksonville, interface visible.

Fly through any scene freely. Click two points to measure the distance on the ground, the height difference, and how sure the model is. It opens at true scale — nothing is stretched.

5 · Confidence — c5a_confidence_jacksonville.mp4

1:46–1:58

Satellite view, switching to the "How sure we are" colours at about 4 s.

Every height comes with a confidence. Warm colours are where the model is unsure — and that confidence is checked against real LiDAR.

6 · Checked against LiDAR — c5b_lidar_compare_omaha.mp4

1:58–2:12

The divider sweeps left and right: our model on one side, the real laser survey on the other.

Drag to compare with real airborne LiDAR. On regions held out from training, we're within 3.5 metres per building; the published figure over Asia is 5.9.

7 · Where we're wrong — c5c_where_wrong_omaha.mp4

2:12–2:22

Error colours on Omaha: blue is too low, red too high.

And we show where we're wrong. Blue is too low — very tall towers are our known weakness, and the viewer says so.

8 · Disaster use case — c6_flood_sikkim.mp4

2:22–2:40

"Flood the valley" is on; the water rises for about 14 s to 1,143 m, then holds on the readout.

Built for disaster management. Raise the water in a Sikkim valley: at 1,143 metres, a fifth of the valley and 44 buildings are under water — and buildings too close to call are flagged.
