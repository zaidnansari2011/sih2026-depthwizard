6.2 The split — region-disjoint, seed 1337

SplitRegionsShard filesRole
train7661 × .npzFine-tuning

test1614 × .npzHeld out. Spent twice by accident before the --split flag existed; not scored since.
Why region-disjoint, when it makes our numbers look worse

Whole regions go to one split. No tile from a training region can appear in validation.

A random tile split leaks: adjacent tiles share buildings, lighting, architecture and shadows, so a model can score well by memorising a neighbourhood. The published HTC-DC Net DFC19 results randomly split 44,258 crops of 256×256 from two cities, which means the crop immediately next to a test crop — same building, same rooftop, same street — is in the training set.

This is why our 4.68 m zero-shot figure and their 2.12 m are not the same number. Presenting our result as losing to theirs would be wrong, and quietly adopting their protocol to close the gap would be worse.

The test split is materially easier than validation

The same zero-shot global-affine baseline reads 4.68 m on test against 9.308 m on validation. The test split also contains exactly one building above 30 m (maximum 34.0 m), while validation reaches 155.3 m. When a test number is finally reported it will look better than validation, and that must be said whenever it is quoted.

Why GAMUS matters and why it is not the only corpus

  • It is 36× our tall-building supervision. Our single measured defect is buildings above 20 m, and the cause is proven data scarcity. Three dense American cities is exactly the missing population.
  • It is the only route to a credible generalisation claim. Training on two cities and testing on the same two cities cannot demonstrate transfer. Training on DFC2019 and holding out a whole GAMUS city can.
  • It is the sponsor's own recommendation, which carries weight independent of its technical merits.
  • Not GAMUS alone, because it is entirely dense urban. Replacing DFC2019 with it would discard our sparse, forested and mixed coverage — three of the four landscapes ISRO explicitly names.

An honesty note that belongs in any report

GLO-30 is itself a surface model, not bare earth, so in dense urban areas it already contains part of the building height we are about to add. SRTM has the same property. --dem-bare applies a coarse low-percentile filter as an approximation and says that it is one.

Training

The recipe, the exact hyperparameters of the shipping model, all six runs with their outcomes, and the thermal problem that governed everything.

7.1 The recipe and where it comes from

Training follows Depth Any Canopy, which is the only published adaptation of Depth Anything to aerial height that reports its hyperparameters: AdamW, backbone learning rate 5×10-6, 5% linear warm-up then linear decay, a handful of epochs.

Two deliberate departures: a Gaussian negative-log-likelihood objective instead of MSE, and a separate, larger learning rate for the head.

7.5 Precision selection

pick_precision() chooses bf16, fp16 or fp32:

Do not use torch.cuda.is_bf16_supported()

It counts emulated bf16 and returns True on hardware that has none. run06's Kaggle log printed “bf16 supported: True” on a Tesla P100, which has no bf16 at all. On a T4 it would likewise pick bf16 and get a slow software path instead of Turing's fp16 tensor cores — the exact opposite of what the function promises. Compute capability is the unambiguous test: bf16 is native from 8.0 onward.

7.7 The thermal problem, and how it was actually solved

Unregulated, the RTX 3060 reached 91 °C in under ten minutes and down-clocked itself from 1875 MHz to 1492 MHz. The “full speed” run was not faster, only hotter. nvidia-smi -pl is the clean fix but needs an administrator shell, so train.py carries a software ThermalGovernor: sample every N steps, pause proportional to overshoot, hard-stop above a ceiling until it cools.

The software governor was a workaround for a settings bug

MSI Afterburner has a Prioritize switch between power and temperature, and it defaults to power — which makes the temperature limit advisory. The card draws to its power limit and lets temperature land wherever it lands. That is why a limit of 77 °C still produced 91 °C readings, and why raising the fan from 62% to 85% bought only about 3 °C.

BeforeAfter (Prioritize = temperature)
Temperature91 °Cmean 80.1 °C, peak 85 °C
Clocksthrottled to 1492 MHz1777–1867 MHz
Throughput53 steps/min143 steps/min
12-epoch run3.6 h~1.6 h

Cooler and 2.7× faster, because the card down-clocks smoothly instead of being stopped and started. Working configuration: fan 85% with auto off, temperature limit 80, power limit 85%, Prioritize = temperature, and the link toggle off so the two limits can be set independently. The software governor now fires zero times and remains only as a backstop.

Never change GPU settings while a CUDA job is running

Applying Afterburner settings resets the display driver and invalidates live CUDA contexts. Doing it mid-run killed a training process with torch.AcceleratorError: CUDA error: unknown error and silently reverted the power limit to 170 W.

7.8 Built for interruption

Kaggle sessions are capped at 12 hours and can be reclaimed sooner, so everything assumes the process may die at any moment:

  • State is checkpointed every epoch and on a wall-clock budget (--max-hours).
  • --resume auto restores model, optimiser, scheduler and RNG state.
  • The metrics log is append-only JSONL, so a partial run still plots.
  • Preprocessing is done locally — it is CPU and disk bound and burns zero GPU quota — then shards are uploaded once as a Kaggle Dataset and every session mounts them instantly.
  • Code lives in git and is cloned into notebooks. Code is never written inside a notebook.

7.9 Preflight — refusing to start a broken run

tools/preflight.py exists because run05 burned nine GPU-hours producing nothing. It measures the initial residual before a run begins, and it is the tool that revealed the 16.5 m against 666 m difference between DA-V2-Small and DA-V1-Large. A long run that is already broken at step zero is now refused rather than discovered six epochs later.

Part 8

Inference

How a full scene of arbitrary size is processed, and every optional stage that can be switched on: augmentation, resolution fusion, zoom matching, DEM anchoring and control points.

8.1 Sliding window

The model accepts exactly 518×518. A real scene does not come in that size, so infer.py tiles it.

Tile size
518 (--tile), which must be a multiple of 14
Overlap
140 px (--overlap), giving a stride of 378
Blending
A cosine window over the overlap region, so neighbouring windows fade into each other rather than producing visible seams
Variance combination
Variances combine in quadrature, not by averaging the σ values. Averaging standard deviations is wrong and would understate uncertainty in the overlaps.
Batch
4 windows at a time (--batch)

A 1024×1024 tile is covered by a 3×3 grid of windows — nine forward passes.

8.2 Test-time augmentation

--tta runs each window eight times under the D4 group — four rotations, each with and without a mirror — then un-transforms and averages the results.

ConfigurationWhole-tile RMSEPer-buildingBiasECE
run02, single pass6.4563.771−0.310.083
run02 + TTA6.3693.616−0.340.078

D4 augmentation is legitimate here in a way it would not be for ground-level photography, because a satellite image has no privileged orientation. Rotating it 90° produces another perfectly plausible satellite image.

8.3 Resolution fusion

--fuse-zoom 2 --fuse-sigma 8 runs a second pass at 2× magnification and fuses it with the first using a Laplacian split: the low-frequency component (smoothed with a Gaussian of σ = 8) comes from the native-resolution pass, and the high-frequency detail comes from the zoomed pass.

A deliberate trade, made because half the marks are visualisation

Fusion costs +0.051 m of per-building RMSE — 1.4% against TTA alone, and buys +28% edge definition: buildings read as flat-topped blocks rather than rounded mounds. It also improves bias and calibration slightly.

The fused numbers are the ones reported, because whatever is shipped must be what is scored. Reporting the better 3.616 m while shipping the 3.667 m configuration would be dishonest by a margin larger than the difference.

8.5 Auto-zoom — surviving a different evaluation resolution

We train at 0.3 m. ISRO will evaluate on their own imagery, likely nearer 0.6–1.0 m. Untreated, that costs a great deal: a backbone token then covers twice the ground, and buildings get averaged with their surroundings.

One token is 14 pixels. At 0.3 m per pixel that is 4.2 m of ground per token. At 0.6 m it becomes 8.4 m — wide enough to swallow a small house whole.

--auto-zoom reads the ground sample distance from the input's geospatial metadata and upsamples so the backbone always sees the scale it was trained at.

Input GSDUntreated per-building RMSEWith --auto-zoomRecovery
0.3 m (native)1.350 m——
0.6 m2.260 m1.399 mwithin 3.6% of never having lost the resolution
1.0 m3.138 m1.657 mroughly half the loss recovered

--native-gsd declares what the model was trained at (default 0.3). If the image has no geospatial metadata there is nothing to read, and the flag has no effect — which is correct behaviour, not a silent failure.

8.6 DEM anchoring — producing an absolute DSM

--dem auto turns above-ground heights into absolute elevation:

DSM = terrain elevation (Copernicus GLO-30) + our above-ground height
Tile discovery
dem.tiles_for_bounds() returns every 1°×1° GLO-30 tile touching the scene's bounding box. A scene straddling a tile edge is normal, not exotic — assuming one tile is how a coastal or border scene ends up with half its elevation missing and no error raised.
Tile naming
Copernicus_DSM_COG_10_N27_00_E088_00_DEM — constructed from the floor of the latitude and longitude.
Access
Read over HTTPS through GDAL's /vsicurl/, windowed, so only the needed bytes are fetched.
Resampling
Cubic, onto the scene's own pixel grid.
Datum handling
Terrain is stored relative to a datum (terrain_datum_m in the manifest), or the mesh sits 1.3 km from the origin and every camera default aims at empty sky.
Bare-earth option
--dem-bare applies a coarse low-percentile filter to approximate removing GLO-30's own buildings and canopy, and labels the result as an approximation.
Default state
Off in the public deployment. --dem auto reaches Copernicus during a request that already takes a minute, so it is opt-in per deployment rather than a surprise timeout.

8.7 Ground control point calibration

The problem statement names “a limited set of Ground Control Points” as an acceptable route to absolute scale. This is implemented and, importantly, measured.

Affine is the wrong functional form

The model's error is a height-dependent ratio — 1.20× at 0–3 m, falling to 0.52× above 40 m. No straight line can be above 1 at one end and below it at the other. Measured head to head on held-out buildings, per tile, over the 10 validation tiles containing a building above 20 m:

FormControl points (k)RMSEvs uncalibratedMAETiles made worse
uncalibrated—7.248 m—4.013 m—
affine86.154 m−15.1%4.477 m8 of 10
power55.730 m−20.9%4.202 m4 of 10
power85.442 m−24.9%4.038 m5 of 10

Affine buys RMSE by spending MAE (+11.6%); the power law takes more RMSE and leaves MAE where it found it (+0.6%). Over all 57 usable tiles the power law is the only form that beats doing nothing at all (−4.4% against affine's +0.1%).

Guards, calibrated against the measurement rather than chosen

  • At least 2 control points must lie in the upper 60% of their height span. Requiring the upper half would refuse 24.6% of the draws that produced the gain; the upper 60% refuses 6.3%.
  • The fitted exponent must be ≥ 0.95, which is what catches “this fit would compress the heights further.”
  • Control values are read as a structure median — region-grown from the clicked point — not a fixed patch. On one Omaha tile a point sample returned 0.59 m where the truth was 5.6 m.
Honest end-to-end status

On the real tiles tested, the guards decline and the system falls back to offset-only. On OMA_288_012 the fitted exponent is 0.40–0.57 because that tile under-predicts everything; on JAX_165_018 it is 0.505 because the model is already good there. Both refusals are correct. It never degrades a result — it helps only when control points carry building-level heights and the scene genuinely spans a height range.

A selection trap worth remembering

The first probe required 25 buildings per tile and looked clean. It was worthless: those 36 tiles contain zero buildings above 20 m (RMSE 1.381 m), while all 60 tall buildings sit in tiles with fewer (RMSE 8.487 m). Dense suburbs have many small buildings and downtowns have few large ones, so a threshold chosen for sample size excluded 100% of the problem.
